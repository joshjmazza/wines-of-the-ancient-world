A tasting in five ports

Wines of theAncient World

An Odyssey across the wine-dark sea

Homer’s sailors crossed a wine-dark sea. Tonight we follow wine itself: out from the Aegean to the Caucasus, down the Phoenician coast, across the Adriatic, and home at last to the mountain where wine began.

I · Santorini II · Georgia III · Lebanon IV · Croatia V · Armenia
I

Setting sail · The Aegean

Santo Wines Assyrtiko

Santorini, Greece

Santorini’s vineyards grow in the ash and pumice left by the Bronze Age eruption that buried the town of Akrotiri. Growers train the vines into low woven baskets called kouloura, which shelter the grapes from the island’s constant wind. Phylloxera never reached the island, so many vines still grow on their own roots. Santo Wines is the island’s growers’ cooperative.

Grape
Assyrtiko
In the glass
Dry and bright, with lemon, salt and crushed stone
Serve
Well chilled, 48–50°F
Pair with
Oysters, grilled fish, feta

II

The edge of the world · The Caucasus

Vazisubani Estate “3 Qvevri”

Kakheti, Georgia

Georgia holds the oldest evidence of winemaking yet found, about 8,000 years old. Here the grapes ferment with their skins in qvevri, large clay vessels buried in the ground, a method UNESCO recognizes as cultural heritage. The skin contact gives this white wine its amber color and gentle grip. The estate dates to 1891 and is tied to the noble Chavchavadze family.

Grapes
Kisi, Mtsvane, Rkatsiteli
In the glass
Amber, with dried apricot, honey, black tea and fine tannin
Serve
Cool rather than cold, about 55°F
Pair with
Roast chicken, mushrooms, walnuts, aged cheese
Visit Vazisubani Estatevazisubaniestate.ge
III

The sailors’ coast · Phoenicia

Château Musar Red

Bekaa Valley, Lebanon

The Phoenicians sailed from this coast and traded wine to every shore of the Mediterranean. Gaston Hochar founded Musar in 1930, and the estate famously kept making wine through Lebanon’s civil war. Musar holds its red back for several years before release, so it arrives already savory and mature.

Grapes
Cabernet Sauvignon, Cinsault, Carignan
In the glass
Medium-bodied, with dried cherry, cedar and spice
Serve
60–64°F. It throws sediment, so stand the bottle upright and pour gently.
Pair with
Lamb, grilled meats, mezze
Visit Château Musarchateaumusar.com
IV

Calypso’s island · The Adriatic

Škaramuča Dingač

Pelješac, Croatia

Dingač’s vines cling to slopes so steep above the sea that growers carried the harvest out by donkey until they cut a tunnel through the hill in the 1970s. Across the channel lies Mljet, which local legend names as Ogygia, the island where Calypso kept Odysseus for seven years. In 1961 Dingač became the first Croatian wine with a protected origin. Its grape, Plavac Mali, is a child of Tribidrag, the variety America knows as Zinfandel.

Grape
Plavac Mali
In the glass
Full-bodied, with dark fruit, currants, dried fig and spice
Serve
60–64°F
Pair with
Grilled lamb, braised meats, hard cheese

V

Homecoming · Ararat

Karas “Grand Karas”

Armavir, Armenia

In Armenia’s Areni-1 cave, archaeologists found the oldest known winery: a press and fermentation jars about 6,100 years old. Karas farms volcanic soil in the Ararat Valley, below the mountain itself, and takes its name from the Armenian word for the clay amphora. Grand Karas is the estate’s flagship red, made from the best grapes of each harvest. Like Odysseus, we end where the story began.

Grapes
Syrah, Cabernet Franc, Montepulciano
In the glass
Deep ruby and full-bodied, with soft tannins and a long finish
Serve
62–65°F. Decant it if you can.
Pair with
Grilled red meats, rich stews, strong cheese
Visit Karas Wineskaraswines.com

Why this order

We open with the brightest wine and build toward the biggest. The Assyrtiko wakes up the palate. The amber 3 Qvevri has the weight and tannin of a light red, so it bridges the whites and reds. Musar comes before the two big reds, because its mature, savory character would read as thin after them. The voyage closes with the Dingač and the Grand Karas, the two richest wines of the night.
